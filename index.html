CPGE RESEARCH PROJECT · TIPE 2026

Thermo-mechanical
coupling in elastomers.

How can cyclic deformation make an elastomer heat up until it becomes unstable? This research project studies the feedback between viscoelastic energy dissipation, temperature rise and the softening of rubber.

The work combines a Zener (Standard Linear Solid) rheological model with the WLF time–temperature relation, then uses Python simulations to explore thermal runaway. It also examines hysteresis measurements, signal filtering and the limits of simplified thermal models.

B / COURSEWORK

Modelling in practice

During my preparatory classes, my TIPE brought together mathematical modelling, thermomechanics and numerical methods to investigate thermal runaway in elastomers. I developed a Zener viscoelastic model with temperature dependence, explored the coupled heat-generation process through Python simulations, and compared the theoretical approach with an experimental setup using a vibrating string and an infrared sensor.

C / REFLECTION

From model to reality

This project taught me that a mathematical model is only as useful as its assumptions and its connection to measurable results. I learned to combine physical reasoning, numerical simulation and experimental analysis, while recognising the limits of a simplified model. The experiment did not produce a measurable temperature rise, which made it important to examine heat losses and measurement constraints rather than force a conclusion. Next, I would explore a more detailed material model, spatial finite-element simulations and a better-controlled thermography experiment.
